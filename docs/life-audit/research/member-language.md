## 1. Who they are

- **English-speaking Africans aged 25 to 40, at home and abroad** (Lagos, Accra, Nairobi, Johannesburg, London, New York, Toronto), with the door open to all Black professionals (Voice Guide). About 3 in 4 people using the app are in Nigeria; the UK diaspora in south and east London is the second real market (dossier, Mixpanel).
- **The 4 interviewees:**
  - **Tola:** early (since-inception) member and product person. Uses Bestday as a goal tracker and rarely posts. Cycles casually.
  - **Anabelle:** based in Ghana for 8 years and was with family in Milton Keynes at the time. Creative project manager and curator who runs Tribe. About 1 month on Bestday. Posts "things I did before 9am" on Instagram.
  - **Favour:** Bestday team member and daily user. Her goals cover money, fitness, community, travel and her relationship with God.
  - **Alex:** London-based. Finished an MSc in June. Left US-hours email-marketing work and is pivoting to the UK civil service. Sets quarterly goals and checks in on Sundays.
- **They already have a system.** Notion boards, Monday.com, Google Docs, a flip chart, a printed list on the office wall, Sunday reviews, quarterly plans.
- **They are solo by default.** Three of the four said so. They accept being held "only because of the result it gets me". Alex is the exception: "I really prefer being a team player."
- **They carry other people.** They are the organised one, "the one chasing, chasing, chasing".
- **They fear looking "not serious".** They show wins and hide struggle, which they share only with a trusted few.
- **They are tired of noise.** "My phone is now becoming quite sacred."
- **They speak polished international English.** None of the 4 interviews contains any Pidgin (dossier).
- **Wider audience (creators, outreach research):** diaspora healthcare and grad-school professionals aged 23 to 35, "whose money and career goals are tied to relocation". Nigeria responds most to **money** framing; UK groups respond to **friendship and belonging** (Outreach Kit).

### Words they never use (in all 4 interviews), and words they reject

**Never said by any member:** wellbeing, wellness, self-care, balance or work-life balance, thrive, flourish, mindset, potential, purpose (as a noun about life), passion, values, alignment, fulfilment as a state (Favour says "more fulfilling" about a result, once), "best self", "level up", grind, hustle, discipline, motivated or motivation, "life areas", "rate", "satisfied", "score", "growth journey", manifest (except to reject manifestation journals), "safe space", "support network". *(Checked against the cleaned transcripts as read; verify against raw transcripts if a word becomes load-bearing.)*

**Said once, with a twist:**
- "journey": Tola says "my own personal journey", but only to say he won't share it in public. The Voice Guide still bans "your journey" in Bestday copy.
- "community": members *do* say it, but they keep it apart from the circle. Tola: "this community of people who are intentional"; Favour: "I cannot put community and circle together." To them, community is a wider, looser thing.
- "co-achievement": only Favour, who is on staff, says it ("That's the whole point. Co-achievement."). The three non-staff members never do.
- "Solo achievement is dead": Favour says it, but the Voice Guide bans it as Bestday copy.

**Explicitly rejected:**
- Being labelled: "If you called me a power user, I might not agree." (Alex)
- Over-measurement: "we live in this optimization world where we're trying to see the metric of everything... it's not that deep." Tola also stopped tracking his cycling routes: "I've stopped doing that too."
- Bookstore planners: "manifestation journals, all these kinds of planners they sell in bookstores. I don't enjoy them." (Favour)
- Building in public: "I think I'm done with that whole building in public." (Tola)
- Strangers in the circle: "No, that's not possible." (Favour)
- Complicated apps: "too complicated and messy" (Anabelle)
- Notifications: "I turn off notifications basically. I hate getting constant notifications." (Tola)
- Anabelle on unwanted pressure: "I don't want anyone to come and serve me."

**Bestday's own banned list (Voice Guide and Brand Language Guide), for cross-checking question copy:** level up, crush your goals, grind, 10x, supercharge, unlock your potential, become your best self, hold space, your journey, show up for yourself, users, community (generic), network, accountability infrastructure, optimise, hack, monitored, tracked (as what people do to you), streak, score, points, badge, no excuses, unserious, motivated, passionate, disciplined. Also: no "blessed", "by grace" or "testimony" in Bestday's voice, and no Pidgin or slang in copy.

## 5. Self-talk register: good times and bad times

**When it's going well, they are matter-of-fact and slightly proud, never gushing.**
- "I worked out this morning and I'm glad I did that." (Anabelle)
- "I'm always at the gym or reading a book or hanging out with community." (Favour)
- "I've established the standard and now I must keep up with it." (Favour)
- "I'm usually much more agile." / "If I'm type A, Alex is type A+." / "an overachiever like me" (Favour)
- "This was a way for me to feel accomplished." (Anabelle)
- Wins are proven with a screenshot, a post or a number, not with adjectives: "That screenshot does all the heavy lifting." (Tola)
- Big wins are "something quite monumental", shared with "this safe community" rather than the internet (Tola).

**When it's going badly, they are plain, a bit wry and quick to shrink the drama. No self-pity and no therapy language.**
- "It's been a bit slower than I would have liked." (Anabelle)
- "I was just feeling fat basically." (Anabelle)
- "I don't enjoy it. I don't feel like I'm doing the goal." (Favour)
- "So it's not consistent." (Tola)
- "I don't think I did last week, if I'm being honest." (Alex)
- "I was waking up early and just not doing enough." (Anabelle)
- "I'm not very good at asking for help." (Anabelle)

**Patterns**
- **Flat verdict, then reason:** "Solo. That's my default... because I think people just slow you down sometimes."
- **Honesty markers before the true thing:** "if I'm being honest", "to be honest", "trust me".
- **Deflating the stakes:** "it's not that deep", "basically", "whatever".
- **Blame goes to circumstances or other people, not to character:** "moving around a lot very recently", "travelling for interviews", "the people are not reliable". They never call themselves lazy or undisciplined. Copy that implies it will lose them.
- **Struggle is named one to one, not broadcast.** Favour deciding to "publicly, properly declare" a struggle is presented as a deliberate, brave exception.
- **They talk in specifics:** 5kg, Q3, four new clients, five minutes before bed, every Monday around 9am, a 15-minute stretch.

## 7. "Never sounds like a member": tells in Bestday's past copy (Swipe File)

Each tell is a real Bestday line that drifted, with what gives it away. None of these sounds like anything the 4 members said.

1. **Borrowed statistics as proof.** "Goals you set with real accountability succeed 95% of the time." / "your odds of achieving go up by 95%". Members prove things with a screenshot or their own number, never a study.
2. **Hustle certainty.** "The math doesn't lie — and most goal apps refuse to look at it." Members hedge ("if I'm being honest") and deflate ("it's not that deep").
3. **System-speak nominalisations.** "the accountability infrastructure that turns ambition into achievements"; "the wider monthly body". Members say "system" and "the people around you".
4. **Internal coinages.** "Coachieve anything", "co-achievement infrastructure", "Ready to co-achieve?". Non-staff members never say it; Tola calls it "the accountability system I basically use".
5. **Combative slogans.** "Solo achievement is dead. Long live co-achievement." Members are solo by default and proud of it.
6. **Status labels that narrow the door.** "High achievers don't go it alone"; "for ambitious people". Members reject labels ("If you called me a power user, I might not agree").
7. **Drill-sergeant or on-demand framing.** "SPARTAN: direct, no-excuses accountability"; "tough love... on demand". Note that Anabelle likes the harsh option ("Some people need that"), but "no excuses" is still banned.
8. **Price or competitor claims.** "...at about 100x lower cost."
9. **Abstract, Latinate belief statements.** "At Bestday, we believe meaningful goals cannot be achieved in isolation."
10. **Generic community.** "Communities: connect with people pursuing similar goals." Members keep community and circle apart.
11. **Gamification.** "Streaks & Celebrations: milestones feel good (because they should)." Members talk about milestones as "something quite monumental", not streaks.
12. **Invented-sounding testimonials with round numbers.** "Lost 30lbs in 4 months...", "Saved $10K in 6 months...", attributed to "Founder, Premium member". Real members give odd, specific numbers (5kg, 15-minute stretch, five minutes before bed).
13. **Developer instructions left in copy.** "Any cohort details, commitments and next steps should be shown before your place is confirmed."
14. **Therapy vocabulary.** "Hold space", "your journey", "show up for yourself".
15. **Long dashes** throughout the shipped copy. The voice removes them.
16. **The right line in the wrong room.** "Bestday is not a book, it is the book club" works internally but means nothing to a stranger.

Gate from the Brand Language Guide, worth applying to every Life Audit question: **"If it could have been written by a habit tracker, rewrite it."** For this quiz, add: if it could have been written by a coach, a wellness app or a personality quiz, rewrite it.

## 8. Implications for the Life Audit (short)

- Members already audit themselves ("monthly or yearly audits", "my own personal research and audit"), so the quiz can treat them as people who have done this before.
- Ask about **specific situations** (a pivot, clothes not fitting, the gym standard, being the one chasing), not about satisfaction scores or feelings in the abstract.
- Use their time units: **this quarter, this week, Sundays.**
- Let them answer with **flat, honest options** ("slower than I'd like", "not consistent", "going well, I post about it") rather than therapeutic or hype scales.
- In questions and answer options, never imply laziness, never call a goal unserious, and never make them say the failure in public. Ask about the goal, not the gap.
- The final "choose one goal" step can mirror Alex: one top sentence for the quarter, plus who gets to see it.
