# R1: Life-assessment instruments and online life audits

Research checked: 10 November 2026. Research only. No site code or reviewed reflection/goal copy changed.

## Decision this research supports

**Recommendation: keep the eight-area wheel, then add a short, substantive exploration of the area the person chooses.** Test that against an evenly covered, 24-item version. More questions can provide better coverage, but question count alone does not establish validity: the Personal Wellbeing Index uses seven domain items, whereas PERMA uses three items per psychological construct. These are different measurement jobs. [PWI author manual](https://www.acc.co.nz/assets/provider/pwi-a-manual.pdf), [PERMA development paper](https://www.internationaljournalofwellbeing.org/index.php/ijow/article/view/526).

The proposals below are product-design recommendations, not validated instruments or approved changes to the build. The current specification requires eight satisfaction ratings, a past-four-weeks frame, missing values for skips, a freely chosen focus, no overall grade, a three-minute promise, and a teaser before email capture. The recommendations preserve those principles unless a departure is identified explicitly. [Current local specification](../SPEC.md), [Life Audit voice](../VOICE.md).

## Scope, method and evidence limits

- **Instrument evidence:** opened author/university pages, original questionnaires/manuals, original development or validation papers, and publisher permission pages. A paper's open-access licence and permission to embed its questionnaire commercially are treated as separate questions. [Kern's current permissions](https://www.peggykern.org/questionnaires.html), [Pearson commercial licensing](https://www.pearsonassessments.com/footer/permissions---licensing.html).
- **Competitor evidence:** opened the named tools, their linked assessment entry points, public methods and sample results. Counts, timing and results described only on a landing page are labelled **vendor claims**. This was not a completed, paid mystery-shop of every funnel. For example, the [Life Audit Typeform](https://form.typeform.com/to/dmhbCsUB) and [Life Audit Score questionnaire](https://lifeauditscore.com/assessment/) did not expose their question sequence to the text browser.
- **US/UK search limitation:** searched `life audit`, `wheel of life`, and UK-qualified variants, plus exact tool names and review queries. Tried country-qualified Google result pages for both terms: [US life audit](https://www.google.com/search?q=life+audit&gl=us&hl=en&pws=0), [UK life audit](https://www.google.co.uk/search?q=life+audit&gl=gb&hl=en&pws=0), [US wheel](https://www.google.com/search?q=wheel+of+life&gl=us&hl=en&pws=0), [UK wheel](https://www.google.co.uk/search?q=wheel+of+life&gl=gb&hl=en&pws=0). Those pages were inaccessible. A localised SERP-provider call required unavailable approval. **No verified US/UK page-one positions or ranking claims are made here.** The competitor set combines search-discovered tools with the founder's requested examples. A reproducible, geolocated page-one census remains unverified.
- **Reading key:** “not verified” means this research could not establish the detail, not that the feature does not exist. Time estimates marked **planning estimate** are my estimates, not measured completion times. Review gaps are search-process findings, not evidence of poor quality.
- **Population:** none of the evidence reviewed establishes validity of Bestday's proposed wording among English-speaking Africans aged 25–40 across the specified cities. Even an established scale needs population-specific checking; Kern explicitly cautions about cultural applicability and carrying norms between populations. [Author guidance](https://www.peggykern.org/questionnaires.html).

## A. Instrument dossiers

### A1. Coaching Wheel of Life and Paul J. Meyer

**Purpose and origin.** This is a coaching conversation aid: select life areas, rate them, draw the shape, discuss priorities. Mind Tools credits Paul J. Meyer and the Success Motivation Institute; PositivePsychology.com dates the attribution to the 1960s. These are contemporary descriptions, not an original dated Meyer manual. The precise invention date was not established from an original source. Do not present “invented in 1960” as a verified historical fact. [Mind Tools](https://www.mindtools.com/ak6jd6w/the-wheel-of-life/), [coaching account](https://positivepsychology.com/wheel-of-life-coaching/).

**Construction.** Common areas include work, finances, health, relationships, enjoyment, surroundings, growth and spirituality, but selection, count and scale vary. The exercise has no universal item wording, scoring rule, diagnostic threshold or completion time. Its usual output is the ratings plotted directly, followed by the participant's interpretation. The sources reviewed did not establish psychometric validation of one canonical Meyer questionnaire. [Coaching formats](https://positivepsychology.com/wheel-of-life-coaching/).

**Bestday implication, proposed.** Borrow the broad visual/reflection idea and write original instructions. Do not treat a round wheel as an empirically established ideal or imply that the lowest area causes the others. Mind Tools itself allows different ideal attention levels by area. Its trademark notice means branded wording/assets need separate clearance. [Mind Tools](https://www.mindtools.com/ak6jd6w/the-wheel-of-life/), [copyright distinction between method and expression](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html).

### A2. PERMA-Profiler, Butler & Kern

**Purpose/evidence.** Measures flourishing across positive emotion, engagement, relationships, meaning and accomplishment. Development reduced a large candidate pool to 15 core items; eight additional indicators produce the 23-item form. The original paper reports development studies with N=7,188 and eight subsequent studies with N=31,966, supporting internal/cross-time consistency and convergent/divergent validity. These are development-sample findings, not proof of Bestday's eight areas. [Original paper](https://www.internationaljournalofwellbeing.org/index.php/ijow/article/view/526).

**Items, anchors and scoring.** Example: “To what extent do you feel loved?” Items use 0–10 with anchors appropriate to frequency, extent or health quality. Mean the three items within each pillar. Health and negative emotion each have three items; loneliness and happiness each have one. The published overall PERMA calculation averages the 15 core items **plus happiness**, not all 23. Sample feedback uses bars. No author timing verified; allow 3–5 minutes for planning. [Author form, pp. 2–3 and 7](https://www.peggykern.org/uploads/5/6/6/7/56678211/the_perma-profiler_101416.pdf).

**Rights/limits.** The PDF routes commercial use to Penn; the current author page routes it to the Wellbeing Lab. Ask the current contact to resolve the route. Kern recommends retaining complete domain sets, warns against universal cutoffs, and says changed scales/populations need psychometric checks. Do not call one borrowed question per area “PERMA validated”. [Current author guidance](https://www.peggykern.org/questionnaires.html).

### A3. Satisfaction With Life Scale, Diener, Emmons, Larsen & Griffin

**Purpose/burden.** Five items assess a global judgement of life satisfaction, not eight needs or goals. The official page gives about one minute. [Scale owner page](https://eddiener.com/satisfaction-with-life-scale-swls/).

**Construction/results.** Example: “I am satisfied with my life.” Responses run from 1, strong disagreement, to 7, strong agreement, with neutral at 4. All five contribute in the same direction. Sum to 5–35; the official form provides seven interpretation bands, with 20 neutral and 31–35 the highest satisfaction band. It supplies no area-specific recommendation. [Official English form](https://eddiener.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/SWLS-Scale.pdf).

**Evidence.** The original development article describes a focused life-satisfaction measure; subsequent large-sample work found single-item life satisfaction correlated with SWLS at r=.62–.64 and had similar associations with external variables. This supports brief global evaluation, not automatic equivalence of a single item for every nuanced domain. [Original article](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327752jpa4901_13), [Cheung & Lucas study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4221492/).

**Rights.** Older Illinois pages say public domain/free professional use; the current Ed Diener page says copyrighted, non-commercial use only. Record the conflict and seek written commercial clarification; do not rely on a third-party “free scale” label. [Older official page](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~ediener/Documents/SWLS.html), [current official page](https://eddiener.com/satisfaction-with-life-scale-swls/).

### A4. Cantril Self-Anchoring Ladder

**Purpose/items.** One global life-evaluation question uses the respondent's own imagined best and worst life. Gallup uses a present rating and a second rating for five years ahead. An example item fragment is “On which step ... do you ... stand at this time?” The range is 0, worst possible life, to 10, best possible life. [Gallup's questions](https://news.gallup.com/poll/122453/understanding-gallup-world-poll-predicts-future.aspx).

**Scoring/presentation.** A single ladder response is already the result. Gallup's separate Life Evaluation Index labels people thriving when present ≥7 and future ≥8, suffering when both ≤4, and struggling otherwise. These categories are Gallup's operational classification, not an intrinsic rule for all 0–10 scales. [Gallup classification](https://news.gallup.com/poll/122453/understanding-gallup-world-poll-predicts-future.aspx).

**Evidence/time.** Internal consistency is inapplicable to one item. The ATS catalogue reports construct-validity evidence in a clinical application, no retest/responsiveness estimate there, and five minutes for the fuller self-anchoring technique. A modern two-tap version should not inherit that timing; under one minute is only a planning estimate. [ATS instrument entry](https://qol.thoracic.org/sections/instruments/ae/pages/cantrils.html).

**Rights.** ATS says no cost, which is not an explicit commercial reproduction licence. Gallup requires permission for its content. Bestday can use an independently written global-evaluation idea if useful, but should not copy Gallup's script or imply a Gallup partnership. [ATS](https://qol.thoracic.org/sections/instruments/ae/pages/cantrils.html), [Gallup permission policy](https://www.gallup.com/corporate/198173/gallup-permissions.aspx).

### A5. WHO-5

**Purpose/format.** Five positively phrased indicators cover good spirits, calmness, vitality, restorative waking and interest in daily life. Example: “I have felt calm and relaxed”. Reference period is **two weeks**. Six frequency choices, paraphrased: 0 none; 1 some; 2 less than half; 3 more than half; 4 most; 5 all. This is a mental-wellbeing measure, not an inventory of work, money or home. [WHO's current form](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mental-health/who-5_english-original4da539d6ed4b49389e3afe47cda2326a.pdf).

**Scoring/evidence/results.** Sum to 0–25, then multiply by four for 0–100. Feedback can be a total and change over time; a wheel is not specified. The developers' 2015 systematic review included 213 articles and supports validity, clinical responsiveness and screening applications. Screening performance is not a diagnosis, and its clinical interpretation would be outside this acquisition tool's purpose. Published timing not verified; 1–2 minutes is a planning estimate. [Developer review](https://doi.org/10.1159/000376585).

**Rights.** WHO's 2024 republication explains the copyright transfer to WHO and applies CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO, with a commercial-permission route. Older descriptions of unrestricted use should not override this edition's explicit terms. Changing its frame to four weeks would also create an adapted administration, not the unchanged WHO-5. [WHO form and licence](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mental-health/who-5_english-original4da539d6ed4b49389e3afe47cda2326a.pdf).

### A6. Harvard Human Flourishing Measure, VanderWeele

**Purpose/items.** Five domains: happiness/life satisfaction, mental/physical health, meaning/purpose, character/virtue, and close relationships. Ten items, two per domain; the 12-item secure version adds financial/material stability. Example: “I understand my purpose in life.” Responses are 0–10, with anchors matched to satisfaction, health, agreement and other constructs; the financial-worry items are oriented so higher means greater security. [Official questions](https://hfh.fas.harvard.edu/measuring-flourishing).

**Scoring/results/time.** Average each pair for domain feedback; overall indices average the constituent items. The programme recommends showing domains separately because their patterns can differ. No completion timing verified; 2–3 minutes is a planning estimate. [Official scoring description](https://hfh.fas.harvard.edu/measuring-flourishing).

**Evidence.** Workplace validation reports alpha .89 for Flourish and .86 for Secure Flourish. A separate cross-cultural study assessed samples from the US, China, Sri Lanka, Cambodia and Mexico. An overall alpha does not show each two-item domain is equally reliable across Bestday's populations. [Workplace validation](https://doi.org/10.1080/23311908.2019.1598926), [cross-cultural validation](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01269/full).

**Rights.** Current programme terms are CC BY-NC 4.0, commercial licensing through Flourishing Hub. The conceptual coverage is useful inspiration; Bestday cannot present a rewritten eight-area version as the Harvard measure. [Official licence](https://hfh.fas.harvard.edu/measuring-flourishing).

### A7. Comprehensive Inventory of Thriving, Su, Tay & Diener

**Coverage/format.** The 54-item CIT has 18 three-item facets: support, community, trust, respect, loneliness, belonging; engagement; skills, learning, accomplishment, self-efficacy, self-worth; control/autonomy; meaning/purpose; optimism; life satisfaction, positive feelings and negative feelings. These sit within seven larger groupings. Example: “I learned something new yesterday”. Agreement runs from 1, strongly disagree, to 5, strongly agree, with neutral at 3. It mixes evaluations, experiences and concrete acts. [Author's instrument](https://eddiener.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/CIT-Scale.pdf).

**Scoring/results.** Compute facet means using the published direction key; reverse control and negative-feeling items when constructing positive-functioning scores. Loneliness is a negative facet and must be labelled/oriented consistently. Retain the facet profile rather than inventing eight life areas from a grand mean. Subscales may be used separately. No published timing verified; 8–12 minutes is a planning estimate. [Instrument and key](https://eddiener.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/CIT-Scale.pdf), [development paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.12027).

**Evidence/rights.** CIT and BIT were evaluated in five US samples totalling 3,191 people, with convergent/discriminant evidence and prediction of health-related outcomes beyond other measures. That does not establish locally representative norms. Copyrighted; free permission is explicitly for non-commercial professional use with credit. [Development abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24919454/), [author permissions](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~ediener/CIT_BIT.html).

### A8. Brief Inventory of Thriving

**Purpose/items.** BIT samples broad thriving with 10 positive statements on purpose, optimism, life going well, positive feeling, worth, efficacy, achievement, energy, being appreciated and belonging. Example: “I am optimistic about my future.” The same 1–5 disagreement/agreement format is used. [Original BIT form](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~ediener/Documents/4.26%20CIT%20and%20BIT/Appendix%20B%20BIT%20Full%20Instrument.pdf).

**Scoring/results/time.** Sum to 10–50 or report the corresponding 1–5 mean, specifying which convention is used. Its principal result is overall thriving, not a reliable separate result for every concept represented by one question. No normative eight-spoke wheel is specified. No timing verified; allow 1–2 minutes for planning. [Development/scoring source](https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.12027), [form](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~ediener/Documents/4.26%20CIT%20and%20BIT/Appendix%20B%20BIT%20Full%20Instrument.pdf).

**Evidence/rights.** Shares CIT's original validation programme and non-commercial restriction. This is a useful demonstration that a short scale can measure an overall construct; it is not evidence that 10 items yield 10 dependable domain diagnoses. [Original development study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24919454/), [author terms](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~ediener/CIT_BIT.html).

### A9. Ryff psychological wellbeing

**Purpose/coverage.** Six dimensions: autonomy, environmental mastery, personal growth, positive relationships, purpose and self-acceptance. There are multiple versions, not one universal count: commonly 84, 54, 42 and 18 items. The Penn overview documents short and longer per-dimension forms. [Penn instrument overview](https://ppc.sas.upenn.edu/resources/questionnaires-researchers/psychological-well-being-scales), [administration account](https://liberalarts.wabash.edu/assessment-notes/ryff-scales-of-psychological-well-being/).

**Items/scoring/output.** Example: “I like most aspects of my personality.” A common format is six-point agreement; reverse the specified negative items, then sum/mean within each dimension. Some study versions change category count/direction, so the chosen version's key matters. Feedback is a six-domain profile, not a goal-archetype classification. No author-standard timing verified. Planning estimates: 3–5 minutes for 18 items, 7–12 for 42–54, and 12–18 for 84. [Administration and examples](https://liberalarts.wabash.edu/assessment-notes/ryff-scales-of-psychological-well-being/).

**Evidence/limits.** Ryff and Keyes' 1995 paper reports only .33–.56 alpha for its three-item dimensions, selected partly for conceptual breadth. A separate Wisconsin measurement analysis questions the six-factor separation. “Validated” does not remove version-specific reliability/factor concerns. [Original 1995 paper, reproduced](https://pubhtml5.com/hzuy/emlr/basic/), [Wisconsin study](https://cde.wisc.edu/wp-2002-09/).

**Rights.** Institutional sources direct requests to Carol Ryff; a no-charge research distribution statement is not a verified commercial grant. Ask about the exact version, commercial embedding and permitted modifications. [Permission contact](https://m3ewb.research.uconn.edu/measure/ryff-scales-of-psychological-well-being/).

### A10. Frisch Quality of Life Inventory

**Purpose/coverage.** QOLI evaluates personally weighted life satisfaction. Its 16 areas are health, self-esteem, goals/values, money, work, play, learning, creativity, helping, love, friends, children, relatives, home, neighbourhood and community. Pearson counts 32 responses and gives approximately five minutes. [Publisher specification](https://www.pearsonassessments.com/store/usassessments/en/p/quality-of-life-inventory/100000635).

**Items/anchors.** For a domain such as health, the respondent judges importance and satisfaction separately. This is a description of the task, not a reproduced proprietary item. Importance has three levels, 0–2; satisfaction has six levels, −3, −2, −1, +1, +2, +3, with no neutral zero. The endpoints express strong dissatisfaction/satisfaction. [Validation paper's method](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1742-9536.2012.00054.x).

**Scoring/results.** Multiply importance by satisfaction, then average the products across domains rated important, excluding importance-zero areas. The raw result spans −6 to +6; licensed norms convert it for interpretation. A published application explicitly describes this exclusion and averaging rule. The short Penn description mentions summation; use the manual for exact missing-data and norm-conversion procedures. Pearson offers overall, weighted-domain and progress reports. **This is not `importance × (10 − satisfaction)`.** [Published scoring application](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.926048/full), [Penn summary](https://ppc.sas.upenn.edu/node/218), [publisher](https://www.pearsonassessments.com/store/usassessments/en/p/quality-of-life-inventory/100000635).

**Evidence/rights.** Original validation reports internal consistency .77–.89 and retest .80–.91 across clinical/nonclinical samples. Those estimates concern QOLI, not arbitrary gap formulas. QOLI is sold as a proprietary assessment; Pearson has a specific commercial-platform licensing route. [Original study record](https://doi.org/10.1037/1040-3590.4.1.92), [commercial permissions](https://www.pearsonassessments.com/footer/permissions---licensing.html).

### A11. Huebner's life-satisfaction scales

**MSLSS.** Forty items cover family (7), friends (9), school (8), living environment (9), and self (7). Example: “I like being in school.” The child format runs from 1, never, through sometimes/often, to 4, almost always. Adolescent use also has six-point agreement, strongly disagree to strongly agree. Reverse keyed items; calculate domain means and a stated overall convention. Results are a school-age satisfaction profile. No universal timing verified; 8–15 minutes is a planning estimate. [Author manual, hosted copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/46791898/huebslssmanual), [measurement study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5112029/).

**Evidence.** Domain-level research exists, but one measurement study found heterogeneity and internal-validity problems for the 40-item model. This is neither a uniformly settled instrument nor a direct model for adults in work/business. [Primary measurement study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5112029/).

**SLSS distinction.** The seven-item SLSS concerns global satisfaction, not five domains. Example: “I would like to change many things in my life”. The six-point version runs from 1, strong disagreement, to 6, strong agreement; reverse two items, then sum or mean. Original studies found a unidimensional structure, acceptable consistency and temporal stability in children. [Original research](https://doi.org/10.1177/0143034391123010), [administration guidance](https://www.corc.uk.net/outcome-measures-guidance/directory-of-outcome-measures/students-life-satisfaction-scale-slss/).

**Rights.** Huebner's university page says these scales are not copyrighted and may be used without charge/permission by interested researchers. Commercial embedding and adult modification are not explicitly addressed. Verify before reproducing; write Bestday's adult items independently rather than merely replacing “school” with “work”. [Author's university statement](https://sc.edu/study/colleges_schools/artsandsciences/psychology/our_people/directory/huebner_scott.php).

### A12. Cummins / International Wellbeing Group Personal Wellbeing Index

**Purpose/coverage.** Seven adult satisfaction domains: living standard, health, achievement, relationships, safety, community connection and future security. An optional spirituality item and separate global-satisfaction question exist. The latter is not simply an eighth core domain. [Developer manual, fifth edition](https://www.acc.co.nz/assets/provider/pwi-a-manual.pdf).

**Items/scoring/output.** Example: “How satisfied are you with your health?” Use 0–10, from no satisfaction to complete satisfaction. Report domains separately or their **unweighted mean**, optionally multiplied by 10 for 0–100 presentation. It is not importance-weighted. The manual reports alpha .70–.85 and retest ICC .84 over 1–2 weeks; those are index-level statistics. No standard timing verified; 1–3 minutes is a planning estimate. [Developer manual, measurement and scoring sections](https://www.acc.co.nz/assets/provider/pwi-a-manual.pdf).

**Applied results.** Australian Unity's implementation compares an individual's result to Australian ranges and offers general advice. Those Australian ranges should not become Bestday's norms. [Research partner's tool description](https://www.australianunity.com.au/wellbeing/the-australian-unity-wellbeing-index).

**Edition/rights limitation.** The opened official-author manual hosted by New Zealand ACC is the 2013 fifth edition. Later research cites a 2024 sixth edition; ACQOL's current distributor/manual URL returned an access challenge, so current edition terms could not be checked. No explicit commercial permission was verified. [Current distributor](https://www.acqol.com.au/instruments), [current manual URL](https://www.acqol.com.au/uploads/pwi-a/pwi-a-english.pdf), [study citing the 2024 edition](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10902-026-01017-3).

### A13. Gallup's five elements

**Framework.** Career is about how one spends the day, not only employment. The other elements are social relationships, finances, physical wellbeing and community. This makes the framework relevant to students, entrepreneurs and unpaid roles as well as employees. [Gallup's original framework description](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/237020/five-essential-elements.aspx).

**Do not confuse products.** The 2014 development paper describes a **50-scored-item Wellbeing Finder**, reporting element scores and a 0–100 composite. A distinct Gallup-Healthways **Well-Being 5 View** uses **10 questions**, with element classifications. “Five elements” is not evidence of five questions, nor of a single freely available questionnaire. [Gallup/Healthways development paper](https://about.sharecare.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/Well-being-5_Science_Paper-2.pdf), [Gallup 10-question report](https://www.gallup.com/file/services/186542/GPI_MilitaryVeterans_2015.pdf).

**Items, anchors and time.** A Gallup-authored workshop sheet gives the example “I like what I do every day.” It distinguishes core/view/life-evaluation sets. This research did not verify a complete current commercial questionnaire, every anchor, scoring weights or administration time. Do not reconstruct them from illustrative public items. [Gallup workshop material, institution-hosted](https://www.csus.edu/administration-business-affairs/human-resources/learning-development/_internal/_documents/five-elements-workshop-materials.pdf).

**Evidence/rights.** The development programme used over 13,000 participants in three samples and reports factor/outcome evidence for its resulting Well-Being 5 measure. That sample should not be misattributed to a new five-question Bestday scale. Items and branded reports are proprietary; Gallup's permission policy requires clearance for reproduced content. [Development paper](https://about.sharecare.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/Well-being-5_Science_Paper-2.pdf), [permissions](https://www.gallup.com/corporate/198173/gallup-permissions.aspx).

## B. What the online tools actually teach us

### B1. Question count and result depth are different decisions

The Life Audit's public offering is materially deeper than one rating per area: 70 questions and a paid narrative based on claimed behavioural signals. Its public sample relates stated relationship preferences to reported choices, rather than explaining a percentage alone. This shows a specificity strategy; it does **not** establish that its inferences or AI analysis are valid. Full question wording, weights and response options remain unverified. [Public offering and sample](https://thelifeaudit.co/).

Life Audit Score goes in another direction: 36 items, ten categories, explicit editorial weights, a grade and a named pattern. Its methods say questions, thresholds and recommendations are product choices, not a validated or population-normed instrument. The public sample shows category comparisons and a reflection plan. Bestday can learn from the method transparency while retaining its own prohibition on grades and labels. [Methods](https://lifeauditscore.com/methodology/), [sample result](https://lifeauditscore.com/sample-report/), [Bestday spec](../SPEC.md).

LifePilot's public six-domain framing spans routines, resources and personal judgements, then leads directly to its planning product. Its Interact entry was not readable, so “six areas” must not be reported as “six questions”. Neither the exact scale nor the email/result sequence was established. [LifePilot](https://lifepilot.co/dotheaudit/).

### B2. The classic wheel is usually followed by a conversation

Noomii's public page describes eight familiar areas: career/business, money, health, family/friends, romance, personal growth, fun/recreation and physical environment. It explicitly advises extreme ratings and starting with the lowest. Those are coaching instructions, not evidence that moderate ratings are invalid or that the lowest area is causally holding back a life. **Proposed Bestday choice:** use clear anchors and free focus selection instead of coaching people towards extremes. [Noomii's instructions](https://wheeloflife.noomii.com/).

Mind Tools' published worksheet concerns **actual versus ideal attention**, allowing both overinvestment and neglect. PositivePsychology.com describes charting satisfaction and then exploring it with a coach, including future-wheel exercises. A standalone acquisition tool must supply some of that next-step reflection itself if it wants more than an attractive chart. This last point is a product inference, not a measured conversion finding. [Mind Tools exercise](https://www.mindtools.com/ak6jd6w/the-wheel-of-life/), [coaching workflow](https://positivepsychology.com/wheel-of-life-coaching/).

Robbins' current landing page promises a hidden gap and under 10 minutes, but the live questions were not exposed. His seven-area workbook rates body, emotions/meaning, relationships, time, work/career/mission, finances, and contribution/celebration. Workbook ratings and older web-quiz descriptions should not be presented as the verified current online implementation. [Current web entry](https://www.tonyrobbins.com/quiz/wheel-of-life), [official workbook](https://timetorisesummit.com/hubfs/PDF/LifeByDesign_Workbook_DIGITAL%20%281%29.pdf?hsLang=en).

### B3. Free utilities already deliver much of the basic wheel

Productive Fish includes eight ratings, optional target ratings, a chosen focus, prompts and image/print output. Dailies includes eight ratings, a lowest-area habit, local saving and export without signup, then links to its app. **Inference:** a wheel alone provides limited product differentiation; original reflection that helps select a feasible goal with trusted people is the more promising Bestday distinction. This is not an SEO-ranking or conversion forecast. [Productive Fish](https://productive.fish/wheel-of-life-assessment/), [Dailies](https://gainaura.app/tools/wheel-of-life), [Bestday result/funnel requirement](../SPEC.md).

## C. Measurement design findings

In this section, **evidence** describes a source's finding. **Proposed implication** is my recommendation for Bestday; it has not been experimentally established for this audience.

### C1. Single-item versus multi-item domains

**Evidence.** Single-item global life satisfaction has respectable evidence: Lucas and Donnellan used four national panels with over 68,000 respondents to separate stable, changing and error variance. Cheung and Lucas found similar external-association patterns for single-item satisfaction and SWLS. Neither study establishes the reliability of Bestday's specific eight domain questions. [Reliability study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23087538/), [validity study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4221492/).

**Evidence.** More items can sample a construct's breadth: PERMA deliberately uses three per pillar, while PWI deliberately uses one satisfaction item per broad life domain. A psychological construct such as purpose is not the same measurement task as asking someone directly about satisfaction with their home. [PERMA development](https://www.internationaljournalofwellbeing.org/index.php/ijow/article/view/526), [PWI manual, conceptual distinction](https://www.acc.co.nz/assets/provider/pwi-a-manual.pdf).

**Proposed implication.** The founder's concern is best understood as insufficient useful detail, not proof that eight ratings are scientifically indefensible. Add questions that explain a goal choice. If the objective becomes a dependable eight-domain profile, use several coherent facets per domain and validate them. Eight different domains are not interchangeable items in one scale: alpha across eight spokes cannot, by itself, validate each spoke. The original studies above illustrate the distinction between a global measure and separate domain measures. [PERMA's explicit domain construction](https://www.internationaljournalofwellbeing.org/index.php/ijow/article/view/526).

### C2. Satisfaction, importance and the meaning of a weighted result

**Evidence.** QOLI combines domain importance with satisfaction. PWI uses unweighted satisfaction. Importance weighting is disputed: Trauer and Mackinnon identify interpretability and psychometric problems with multiplicative composites; a later empirical study found no validity improvement from individual importance weighting. Therefore “weighted” is not inherently more scientific. [QOLI description](https://ppc.sas.upenn.edu/node/218), [weighting critique](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11822791/), [empirical comparison](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5892437/).

**Proposed implication.** Keep three meanings separate: satisfaction with the past four weeks, importance of an area in one's life, and readiness to give it attention next month. An area can be very important and already satisfactory; another can be unsatisfactory but outside one's control. Ask about the decision we need rather than multiplying everything into an overall life value. This is a decision-design recommendation informed by the weighting debate, not a validated new formula. [Weighting critique](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11822791/).

### C3. Behavioural items versus evaluative items

**Evidence.** The instruments do not support a blanket rule that behaviour questions are superior for all purposes. SWLS measures evaluations; WHO-5 measures recent positive experience; CIT includes specific behaviours as well as judgements. Those formats answer different questions. [SWLS form](https://eddiener.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/SWLS-Scale.pdf), [WHO description](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/who-ucn-msd-mhe-2024.01), [CIT form](https://eddiener.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/CIT-Scale.pdf).

**Proposed implication.** Use satisfaction to draw the wheel. Use one or more concrete acts to understand the chosen area and select an attainable first step. Do not average exercise frequency, income, tidy rooms and relationship satisfaction into one allegedly comparable number. A behaviour is not automatically an outcome or a deficit: spending little can reflect constrained resources; resting can be a chosen priority. This interpretation follows the distinction between evaluative, affective and functioning measures in the OECD guidance. [OECD measurement guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK189566/).

An original draft example for the focus follow-up is: “In how many of the past four weeks did you make time to speak with someone you wanted to stay close to?” Response buttons: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 weeks; plus “This does not fit my situation”. That yields a concrete detail for reflection without claiming to measure relationship quality. It is proposed Bestday wording, not an existing validated item.

### C4. Frequency, agreement and 0–10 scales

**Evidence.** The 2025 OECD guidelines recommend 0–10 numerical scales with verbally anchored endpoints for most recommended subjective-wellbeing questions. This supports Bestday's evaluative scale; it does not make every quantity an 11-point construct. A randomised multitrait/multimethod study found lower-quality responses for agree/disagree questions than comparable item-specific questions. [OECD 2025 guidance](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/10/oecd-guidelines-on-measuring-subjective-well-being-2025-update_b957f42e/9203632a-en.pdf), [Saris et al.](https://web.stanford.edu/dept/communication/faculty/krosnick/docs/2010/2010_srm_saris_comparing.pdf).

**Proposed implication.** Keep 0–10 for satisfaction. For behaviour use a meaningful frequency or count, with the period in the question. Use distinct single-choice options for barriers and priorities. Introduce scale changes clearly, with consistent direction. Avoid replacing every question with “I agree that...” solely to make the interaction uniform. Endpoint choices must match the construct; “very satisfied” from the current spec is a product choice, not the identical “completely satisfied” formulation in the OECD recommendation. [Current spec](../SPEC.md), [OECD scale guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK189566/).

### C5. Positive and negative items

**Evidence.** WHO-5 uses only positive items. PERMA measures negative emotion separately. Reverse-keyed statements are not synonymous with measuring a negative experience: the former changes scoring direction, the latter may be a distinct construct. In van Sonderen and colleagues' study, reverse wording did not prevent response bias and introduced confusion/inattention. [WHO developer review](https://doi.org/10.1159/000376585), [PERMA key](https://www.peggykern.org/uploads/5/6/6/7/56678211/the_perma-profiler_101416.pdf), [reverse-wording experiment](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0068967).

**Proposed implication.** Include useful questions about friction when needed, but write them plainly. Avoid grammatical traps such as disagreeing with a negative statement. A barrier answer can personalise the goal without being reversed into the satisfaction mean. The older quiz playbook's strength/cost balancing rule should not be mechanically imported into a domain-satisfaction scale. [Local playbook](../../../.claude/skills/bestday-quiz/SKILL.md), [Life Audit scoring exception](../SPEC.md).

### C6. “How do we avoid everyone answering 7?”

**Evidence.** Wellbeing ratings often skew positive; a seven is not intrinsically invalid. Kern warns that middle population responses often fall above the numerical midpoint and that thresholds should not become fixed identities. Noomii's advice to choose extremes is an instruction from a coaching tool, not a corrective statistical procedure. [Kern's interpretation guidance](https://www.peggykern.org/questionnaires.html), [Noomii's instructions](https://wheeloflife.noomii.com/).

**Proposed implication.** Start with no preselected value, retain neutral wording, show the reference period and endpoints, and offer skip/not applicable. Describe a concrete facet before asking for a rating. Inspect pilots for misunderstood terms, ceiling effects and rapid repeated taps, but do not force an even distribution or reject a person because all areas are honestly seven. A tie is a valid product state: show tied areas and ask the person to choose. General concerns about repeated identical answers should prompt checking, not an automatic claim of dishonesty. [OECD 2025 guidance on response sets](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/10/oecd-guidelines-on-measuring-subjective-well-being-2025-update_b957f42e/9203632a-en.pdf).

### C7. Past-four-weeks framing

**Evidence.** There is no universal validated recall period across these measures: WHO-5 uses two weeks; PERMA's original administration concerns general functioning; PWI asks current life satisfaction without a uniform four-week window. Changing the period changes the administration and may change what respondents retrieve. [WHO](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/who-ucn-msd-mhe-2024.01), [PERMA author guidance](https://www.peggykern.org/questionnaires.html), [PWI form](https://www.acc.co.nz/assets/provider/pwi-a-manual.pdf).

**Proposed implication.** Retain four weeks for Bestday's original questions because it matches the current spec and a month of goal planning. Keep that frame visible at section transitions. Use a four-week count for recurring weekly behaviours, and do not ask an occasional behaviour as though everyone had the same opportunity. Four weeks is an operational choice to pilot, not a scientifically superior window established here. A subsequent four-week review also avoids deliberately overlapping the entire recall period. [Current design choice](../SPEC.md).

### C8. Accuracy, specificity and the Barnum effect

**Evidence.** Forer's original personal-validation demonstration showed why endorsement of a description is not independent evidence that it differentiates the person. A flattering, broadly applicable narrative can feel accurate. Therefore testimonials such as “it felt personal” and independently established measurement validity are different evidence. [Forer, original paper record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18110193/), [original paper scan](https://www.astronomy.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Forer-fallacy-of-personal-validation-1949.pdf).

**Proposed implication.** Every result sentence should be traceable to an answer, an explicitly labelled interpretation, or a suggestion. For example, if the person rates relationships 4, chooses making time as the issue, and reports two weeks of contact, report that combination before suggesting a weekly conversation. Do not infer fear of intimacy, avoidance or a psychological identity. Begin with what is working; invite correction. A user should be able to say a sentence is wrong without invalidating their result. This is a proposed safeguard against the personal-validation problem, not a tested Bestday accuracy intervention. [Forer](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18110193/), [Bestday voice](../VOICE.md).

**Proposed validation check.** In a consented pilot, compare an answer-linked result with a generic or mismatched result. Ask which specific sentences fit and why, not only for an overall accuracy rating. Measure correct recognition and correction, then separately measure whether the person chose a feasible goal. This tests usefulness and discrimination more directly than collecting flattering testimonials. The rationale comes from Forer's finding; the study design is a new proposal. [Forer](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18110193/).

### C9. Recommending what to work on

The following algorithms are **candidate product rules**, not validated clinical or wellbeing scores. QOLI's evidence cannot be borrowed to validate a reversed gap heuristic. [QOLI's actual method](https://ppc.sas.upenn.edu/node/218).

| Candidate rule | Explicit definition | Useful property | Honest limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lowest satisfaction | Suggest rated areas with minimum `S`. | Requires no new importance questions. | A low rating does not establish desire, feasibility or causal leverage. |
| Importance-weighted gap | Ask importance `I` on 0–10; calculate `G = I × (10 − S)`. | Makes the importance-versus-satisfaction trade-off visible. | Range 0–100 is arithmetic, not percent need, risk or probability. Assumes target satisfaction is 10 and treats ordinal choices as interval/product scales. |
| Lowest among important | Ask for up to three areas that matter for the coming month; within that set, suggest lowest `S`. | Understandable and avoids implying that all areas should reach 10. | “Important next month” differs from general life importance; selecting three is a task constraint, not validation. |
| Chosen focus | Show all rated areas; ask which one the person wants to give attention to. | Fits the current spec and preserves agency. | Provides no independent evidence that the choice will maximise wellbeing. |

Worked hypothetical example: money `S=3, I=4` gives `G=28`; relationships `S=6, I=10` gives `G=40`. A gap rule recommends relationships despite money's lower satisfaction. If all importance ratings equal 10, gap ordering reduces to lowest satisfaction. If either input is missing, the gap is missing, not zero. These are mathematical consequences of the proposed formula, not participant data.

**Recommendation.** Default to the person's choice. If an automated suggestion is shown, explain its inputs and let it be overridden. For ties, show the tied set; use stable display ordering rather than inventing precision. Never turn a zero importance response into “this area does not matter in your life”. Avoid percentages, risk labels and “the one thing holding everything back”. The weighting literature gives no basis for those stronger interpretations. [Empirical weighting comparison](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5892437/), [weighting critique](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11822791/), [Bestday rule](../SPEC.md).

### C10. Retest and change over time

**Evidence.** Reliability, retest stability and responsiveness are different properties. A measure may be consistent without detecting meaningful change; genuine change can reduce retest correlation. WHO-5 has responsiveness evidence from clinical trials, while single-item life-satisfaction research explicitly separates occasion-specific true variance from error. Neither establishes the minimum meaningful change for Bestday's wheel. [WHO developer review](https://doi.org/10.1159/000376585), [longitudinal reliability study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23087538/).

**Proposed implication.** Preserve wording, anchors, recall period and item order/version across repeat visits. Compare each area with the person's own earlier response, label dates, and keep skipped responses missing. A move from 5 to 6 is a changed answer, not proof of improvement caused by Bestday. Track the goal's concrete action separately from satisfaction; the two may move differently. Context questions can help a person interpret change without entering the rating formula. These are proposed applications of the distinction between evaluation and change measurement. [OECD design/interpretation guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK189566/), [current privacy/scoring rules](../SPEC.md).

**Proposed pilot.** Use cognitive interviews across home/diaspora contexts, then a quantitative pilot with a short retest subset reporting no major change and a later four-week follow-up. Examine missingness, item distributions, same-domain relationships, retest agreement and whether the chosen action happened. For multi-item domains, examine structure and consistency before applying inherited norms or cutoffs. Agreement with a respondent's focus is usefulness evidence, not a complete validation programme. [Population/version cautions](https://www.peggykern.org/questionnaires.html).

**Proposed cultural checks.** In those interviews, test whether work includes study, business and unpaid responsibilities; whether relationships includes people across countries without assuming a partner; whether money wording respects commitments to others; and whether home wording fits shared or multigenerational arrangements. Treat faith as optional, and ask whether the suggested action is within the person's present control. These are design questions for the stated audience, not claims about how all Africans live. [Audience and cultural requirements](../VOICE.md), [inclusive area definitions](../SPEC.md).

## D. Completion, perceived length and mobile

### D1. What the quantitative evidence actually says

| Source / evidence type | Reported finding | What it can support here | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Typeform lead-form analysis](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/4406587604116-Research-driven-tips-to-power-up-your-lead-capture-forms), observational vendor data | Over 1,000 forms; forms with more than six questions had under 50% completion; forms under a minute averaged 15 percentage points higher completion. Email-first associated with +9 points in similar forms. | Burden and gate placement are testable, consequential choices. | Six is not a universal ceiling for a self-discovery quiz; associations are not randomised causal effects. Email-first conflicts with Bestday's already-chosen teaser-first design. |
| [SurveyMonkey 100,000-survey analysis](https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/survey_questions_and_completion_rates/), observational, 2009–2010 | More questions associated with more drop-off; the incremental pattern was nonlinear. “Started” required submission of a page with an answer; one-question surveys therefore had 100% completion by definition. | Count questions and understand the denominator before comparing benchmarks. | Those rates are not landing-to-finish rates, and the old dataset is not a modern mobile forecast. |
| [SurveyMonkey Audience analysis](https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/tips-increasing-survey-completion-rates/), over 25,000 surveys on its research panel | Average completion: 10 questions 89%, 20 87%, 30 85%, 40 79%. Easy multiple-choice opening 89% versus open-text opening 83%. | Per-question difficulty matters; an easy start is a reasonable design hypothesis. | Paid-panel participants are not cold organic visitors. Do not forecast a two-point penalty for doubling Bestday's length. |
| [Interact's 2026 report](https://www.tryinteract.com/blog/quiz-conversion-rate-report/), vendor portfolio benchmark | 65% start-to-finish; 40.1% start-to-lead. Coaching/course subgroup: 59.1% finish, 44.9% lead. | Separate answering all questions from submitting contact details. | No causal question-count curve; not a target guaranteed for Bestday or an independently audited industry norm. |
| [Outgrow / GPYP case](https://outgrow.co/blog/interactive-marketing-success-gpyp), selected marketing case | Nine questions using multiple choice, dropdowns and sliders. Reports 37% overall conversion, 60% of starters supplying leads, and near-perfect completion without supporting completion counts. | Shows a concrete quiz-to-consulting funnel and why metric definitions matter. | The page does not reconcile the conversion denominators or provide an auditable completion rate. No controlled evidence for the best length. |
| [Outgrow / Noom case](https://outgrow.co/blog/interactive-case-study-noom), selected marketing case | Claims 100K+ visitors to an ROI calculator intended to increase engagement/conversion. | Interactive utility can be part of acquisition; relevant as a funnel example only. | This is not evidence that Noom's long consumer health-onboarding quiz completes well. The cited case supplies no usable count-versus-completion experiment. |
| [Galesic & Bosnjak, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1093/poq/nfp031), experiment | Stated lengths of 10, 20 and 30 minutes were manipulated with question-block position. Longer announced length reduced starting/completing; later responses became faster, shorter and more uniform. | Perceived length affects participation and late-question quality, not only finishing. | Does not identify the best point between a three-minute and five-minute Bestday audit. |

**Synthesis.** These results do not yield a defensible universal rule such as “each extra question costs X%” or “20 questions is optimal”. Their audiences, tasks, incentives and denominators differ. Bestday should compare candidate flows on its own traffic and use timing estimates as hypotheses. [Typeform](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/4406587604116-Research-driven-tips-to-power-up-your-lead-capture-forms), [SurveyMonkey's stated limitations](https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/survey_questions_and_completion_rates/), [Interact's definitions](https://www.tryinteract.com/blog/quiz-conversion-rate-report/).

### D2. Progress indicators and sectioning

**Evidence.** A meta-analysis of 32 randomised experiments found no significant overall drop-off benefit for constant progress indicators. Early-fast/later-slow indicators reduced drop-off, while early-slow/later-fast increased it. This contradicts the blanket claim that any progress bar improves completion. A separate experimental programme also treats progress as information that changes expectations about remaining effort. [Villar, Callegaro & Yang](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/14427/), [Conrad et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20676386/).

**Proposed implication.** Retain a truthful progress indicator because it helps orientation and meets the spec, without promising an uplift. For an adaptive flow, distinguish “Your wheel” from “Your focus” rather than displaying an inaccurate total. Tell the person that four follow-up questions remain when the branch starts. Do not reset a bar after it reaches 100%, hide a surprise long section or fake rapid progress. Use short section labels, avoiding several extra interstitial screens. These are design recommendations informed by expectation effects, not a proven optimal section layout. [Progress evidence](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/14427/), [Bestday progress requirement](../SPEC.md).

Typeform recommends explaining why a question series is being asked. This is provider guidance rather than isolated experimental proof that sectioning improves quiz completion. **Proposed implementation:** one short transition before the focus questions, with its purpose stated; no added reading before every item. [Typeform question-design guidance](https://www.typeform.com/blog/survey-school-1-forms-and-questions).

### D3. Mobile burden is more than question count

**Evidence.** Pew's mobile-survey guidance describes additional navigation, typing and rendering difficulties, and recommends mobile-compatible layouts rather than assuming a desktop survey fits. A published experiment directly compares grids versus item-by-item presentation on PCs and smartphones, illustrating why layout and device belong in measurement design. [Pew mobile guidance](https://www.pewresearch.org/methods/2015/06/11/tips-for-creating-web-surveys-for-completion-on-a-mobile-device/), [layout/device experiment](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0736585316300776).

**Proposed Bestday requirements.** At 390px, use readable tap choices and visible anchors; test a two-row 0–10 control if one row is cramped. Provide Back/edit, visible skip, and no prefilled slider value. Avoid a matrix of eight areas by eleven columns. Keep typing for the existing capture/goal-edit step, and test slower connections and interruptions. Retain text values alongside the wheel so interpretation does not depend on reading a small chart. These are proposed interaction choices, not implemented changes. [Pew's underlying usability concerns](https://www.pewresearch.org/methods/2015/06/11/tips-for-creating-web-surveys-for-completion-on-a-mobile-device/), [local mobile requirement](../SPEC.md).

### D4. What to measure in Bestday's experiment

**Proposed definitions:** report landing-to-start, start-to-last-answer, start-to-teaser, teaser-to-email, start-to-chosen-goal and goal-to-app-click separately. A completed assessment and a captured lead are different events; retain both. Capture latency and drop-off by step/section, device and acquisition source. Compare median and slower-completer timing, not only an average among finishers. Report missing/skipped-item rates separately. Interact's distinction between finish and lead illustrates the denominator issue. [Interact definitions](https://www.tryinteract.com/blog/quiz-conversion-rate-report/).

**Proposed success criterion:** favour the structure that produces more credible, feasible commitments per visitor, provided its result is understood and feels properly grounded. Choose an acceptable completion/time trade-off before running the test; no numerical uplift is forecast here. Preserve the current analytics restriction: no ratings, free text, names, emails or phone numbers in Mixpanel events. Any research dataset for validation needs a separate consented collection, not an expansion of acquisition analytics. [Current analytics/privacy specification](../SPEC.md).

## E. Candidate Bestday structures

All counts below are **answer-bearing questions before the teaser**, not screens. They include context, focus, time and a proposed support question; they exclude first name/email, optional WhatsApp, and the later goal/horizon/day actions. Each area retains a skip option. Counts are full-coverage maximums where skipping affects follow-ups. Times are untested planning estimates, not promises ready for the landing page. Current approved copy remains “three minutes”. [Current flow and promise](../SPEC.md).

### E1. Candidate A: eight-area wheel, four focused follow-ups

**16 questions maximum; three sections. Proposed first experiment.**

1. **Take stock, 9 questions:** context + eight current satisfaction ratings, 0–10, past four weeks.
2. **Explore one area, 5 questions:** choose focus + four area-specific questions: which aspect needs attention, one recent behaviour frequency, the practical barrier, and the change the person wants next month.
3. **Make it workable, 2 questions:** weekly time + what help from a trusted person would be useful. The help question includes “I'm not sure” and skip; it requests no name or contact details.

**Scale and scoring, proposed.** The wheel plots the eight original ratings directly. Focus follow-ups use single-choice aspects/barriers/outcomes and a bounded frequency question. They tailor the interpretation and goal selection; they do not retroactively alter the wheel. No overall number, grade, personality label or diagnosis. A skipped area cannot become the focus. If all areas are skipped, follow the spec's return path. [Existing scoring/focus constraints](../SPEC.md).

**Result, proposed.** Show what is working, the chosen area, the specific aspect, one concrete recent observation and the barrier selected. Then offer an achievable goal, a first action and a form of help from the person's circle. Preserve the reviewed reflections/goals; map new answers to existing suggestions or write separately reviewed additions later. Keep the wheel and short reflection free before the existing gate. [Result/gate and reviewed-copy requirements](../SPEC.md), [repo instructions](../../../AGENTS.md).

**Illustrative original branch, relationships.** These are research drafts, not approved copy or validated items:

- Aspect: “Which part would you most like to give attention to?” Choices could distinguish making time, speaking honestly, meeting people, or another aspect.
- Behaviour: the four-week contact-frequency example in C3.
- Barrier: “What has made this difficult lately?” Choices could include conflicting schedules, distance, uncertainty about how to begin, or something else.
- Desired change: “What would you most like to be different next month?” Choices could include more regular contact, one honest conversation, or spending time with someone new.

**Trade-offs, judgement.** Estimated 3–4 minutes, to be timed. Strongest direct fit for choosing one goal and tailoring support, with limited added burden. The other seven domains remain single-item snapshots, so this does not solve a requirement for a psychometrically deep profile of every domain. Branches require careful writing and equal effort across areas. The four-minute end of the estimate would miss the current promise; pilot before release. Its SEO value is a useful wheel plus a substantive action result, not extra questions for their own sake. Google's guidance favours original, helpful content, not a preferred word count. [SEO guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

### E2. Candidate B: three evaluative facets per area, then a goal bridge

**30 questions maximum; three sections. Proposed depth comparator.**

1. **Context, 1 question.**
2. **Life areas, 24 questions:** three distinct satisfaction judgements in each of eight areas, all 0–10 over four weeks. Ask one item at a time, with short area labels. The domains below are an original coverage proposal, not borrowed scale items.
3. **Focus and next step, 5 questions:** focus choice, one concrete recent behaviour in that area, the practical barrier, weekly time and useful support from a trusted person.

| Existing area | Proposed facets for independent item writing |
| --- | --- |
| Work and direction | Direction of work/study/business; progress on meaningful work; fit of everyday responsibilities. |
| Money | Everyday arrangements; ability to handle foreseeable commitments; room to make choices that matter personally. |
| Health and energy | Energy for daily life; fit of physical routines; ability to care for one's physical needs within current circumstances. |
| Relationships | Quality of connection; time with people who matter; ability to ask a trusted person for help. |
| Learning and growth | Opportunities to learn; progress in something personally chosen; room to explore. |
| Rest and enjoyment | Recovery; room for enjoyment; ability to protect some time away from obligations. |
| Home and everyday life | Support from surroundings; manageability of daily arrangements; fit of routines with present needs. |
| Meaning and contribution | Sense that activities matter; alignment with chosen values; ability to contribute in ways personally valued. |

**Scale/scoring, proposed.** Mean the three coherent satisfaction items within an area, displaying the method plainly. Exclude the follow-up behaviour from the mean. Require at least two answered items for a plotted area and label partial coverage; otherwise leave it missing. This two-of-three rule is a proposed product convention, not a rule imported from PERMA. Check whether items actually belong together before treating the mean as a scale. The choice of three is informed by PERMA's construction, but does not transfer PERMA's validation. [PERMA development](https://www.internationaljournalofwellbeing.org/index.php/ijow/article/view/526).

**Result, proposed.** The wheel shows area means, with a readable facet breakdown on request. If facets disagree, describe the difference using the person's answers; do not conceal it behind the mean. Explain why their chosen focus matters to them, then use the behaviour/barrier/time answers for the goal bridge. Retain the same teaser/gate and no overall grade. Averaging within a domain is an explicit departure from the current direct-rating spec. [Current scoring contract](../SPEC.md).

**Trade-offs, judgement.** Estimated 5–7 minutes. Best candidate for more even coverage and a later validation programme. Higher credibility potential, but also greater risk of repetitive items, construct overlap and mobile fatigue. More points do not guarantee better measurement; Ryff's short-domain difficulties show the need to evaluate each constructed subscale. It also adds work that may not affect the single chosen goal. It cannot honestly ship with the present three-minute claim unless observed timing supports that. Strong SEO explanation potential through transparent method and facet examples, with no established ranking advantage over Candidate A. [Ryff's short-form findings](https://pubhtml5.com/hzuy/emlr/basic/), [Google's content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

### E3. Candidate C: importance and satisfaction, then a focused plan

**24 questions maximum; four sections. Proposed decision-focused alternative.**

1. Context, 1 question.
2. Eight satisfaction ratings, 0–10 over four weeks.
3. Eight importance ratings, 0–10, explicitly about what matters to the person in their life. Do not silently label these “readiness”.
4. Focus choice, four focused follow-ups as in Candidate A, weekly time and useful support, 7 questions.

**Scoring/result, proposed.** Keep satisfaction on the wheel. Show importance separately in a simple list. If a suggestion is wanted, use the explicitly described `I × (10 − S)` heuristic, show its inputs, call it a suggestion, and let the person select any rated area. Do not plot weighted products as satisfaction. The result explains either why a highly important unsatisfactory area was suggested or why the person's own choice takes precedence. Missing importance or satisfaction means no calculated gap. This is a departure from the current spec's absence of importance ratings and automated gap guidance. [Existing scoring/focus rules](../SPEC.md).

**Trade-offs, judgement.** Estimated 4–6 minutes. Better visibility of personal priorities and potentially useful discussion when lowest satisfaction is not the person's concern. It still has only one satisfaction item per domain, can produce “everything is important” responses, adds eight repetitive judgements, and imports assumptions about a target of 10. It is a decision aid, not QOLI or a validated improvement over unweighted ratings. Given the evidence against automatic importance weighting, I would test it after A versus B, rather than make it the default. [Weighting evidence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5892437/).

**SEO/funnel fit, judgement.** A public explanation of importance versus satisfaction could be useful for “life audit” searches. It has no demonstrated ranking or conversion benefit, and the extra ratings should earn their place by changing the quality of the eventual goal decision. A simpler alternative is C9's top-three priority selection, which would require fewer questions but would be a different design from this numeric-importance candidate. [Google's usefulness criterion](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), [weighting critique](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11822791/).

### E4. Recommendation and decision criteria

**Choose Candidate A for the first prototype; compare it with Candidate B if the founder wants deeper coverage everywhere.** A addresses the funnel's actual job: help someone identify one worthwhile goal and how trusted people can help. B is the stronger research investment if Bestday wants a repeatable multidomain measure. C makes a prioritisation hypothesis explicit but has the weakest case for treating its extra arithmetic as measurement credibility. These are recommendations based on the trade-offs above, not an evidence-backed winner for an unrun experiment. [Instrument comparison: PERMA](https://www.internationaljournalofwellbeing.org/index.php/ijow/article/view/526), [weighting comparison](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5892437/), [Bestday's goal requirement](../SPEC.md).

Before adopting either, pilot comprehension and phone timing across the intended home/diaspora audience. Decide whether the three-minute promise is a hard constraint. If so, Candidate A needs observed timing and may need one fewer follow-up; B is a likely separate deeper mode with an honestly revised promise, requiring a future spec decision. Do not silently build these departures. The user requested research only. [Current promise](../SPEC.md).

For SEO, publish an original explanation of how to do a life audit, the eight areas, an example wheel with clearly illustrative answers, the method/limits, privacy, and the transition from a wheel to a goal. Keep personal answers out of URLs and indexing. Question count is not a substitute for a useful indexable page; Google's guidance specifically values original usefulness and says it has no preferred word count. [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), [Bestday indexing/privacy requirements](../SPEC.md).

1. A wheel is a flexible coaching exercise, not one universally validated questionnaire. [Coaching description](https://positivepsychology.com/wheel-of-life-coaching/)

3. Short measures can be credible; single-item global life evaluation has substantial evidence. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23087538/)

6. Do not confuse satisfaction, behaviour, importance and readiness. [OECD measurement guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK189566/)
7. Importance-weighted gaps are decision heuristics, not validated QOLI scores. [Weighting research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5892437/)
8. Recommend attention transparently, while letting the person choose the focus. [Bestday requirement](../SPEC.md)
9. Positive wording can work; reverse wording can introduce confusion. [Experiment](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0068967)
10. Make results answer-specific; feeling recognised is not proof of validity. [Forer](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18110193/)
11. There is no universal question-count-to-completion formula. [SurveyMonkey limitations](https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/survey_questions_and_completion_rates/)
12. Progress bars do not reliably reduce drop-off by themselves. [Meta-analysis](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/14427/)
13. First test the proposed 16-question focused flow against the proposed 30-question domain flow. [Structures](#e-candidate-bestday-structures)
14. Write original commercial-use items unless the scale's actual permissions clear reuse. [Rights inventory](#f-what-bestday-may-adapt-and-what-needs-permission)
