# Life Audit v3: structure (proposal for Hachi)

Built from `research/R1-instruments.md`, `research/R2-landing-and-flow.md` and `research/member-language.md`. Replaces the 8-question flow in SPEC.md once approved.

## What the research says, in four lines

1. **Credibility comes from specific questions and a result visibly built from your answers, not from length.** The ranking competitors ask 36 (Life Audit Score) to 70 (The Life Audit) questions; the classic wheel tools ask 8 ratings and feel like a toy. A thin wheel is already free everywhere.
2. **Single 0-10 satisfaction ratings are the weakest choice for us.** They invite everyone to answer 7, they measure a feeling instead of a situation, and "rate" and "satisfied" are words no Bestday member uses.
3. **Members describe their lives as concrete situations in flat, honest words:** work eating into personal time, the side business, clothes that no longer fit, being the one chasing friends, the goal they don't post about, "a bit slower than I would have liked".
4. **Nobody in the category asks about the people around the goal.** That is Bestday's whole idea and the one place the audit can say something no competitor can.

## The proposal: a real audit, about 8 minutes, in four parts

| Part | What it asks | Questions | Format |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Context | What has you taking stock right now | 1 | one tap |
| 1. Where things are | 8 areas x 4 situational statements, written the way members talk | 32 | 5-point "Not me" to "That's me", one tap, auto-advance; skip an area |
| 2. How it's going, in a word | One flat check at the end of each area: "Going well, I'd post about it" / "Fine" / "Slower than I'd like" / "Not where I want it" / "Not on my mind right now" | 8 | one tap |
| 3. What matters this quarter | Pick up to 3 areas that matter most this quarter | 1 | multi-select |
| 4. Who's in it with you | 6 statements about the people around your goals: who knows, who checks, being the one chasing, the goal you won't tell anyone yet, slipping quietly, coming back | 6 | same 5-point scale |
| 5. Your one thing | Suggested focus with the reason shown, you choose; your goal in one sentence (suggestions or your own); first step and day; who gets to see it | 4 | taps plus one short text |

About 52 answers. Most are a single tap on the same scale, so the pace stays fast: roughly 8 minutes on a phone (to be timed in testing). Say "about 8 minutes" everywhere. Grouping by area with a section title gives the sense of progress; R1 found progress bars alone do not reduce drop-off, sections do more.

Item writing rules (all parts):
- Each area's 4 statements: 2 describe something working, 2 describe a cost, so agreeing with flattering lines cannot decide the wheel. Never reverse-word confusingly.
- Situations, not feelings. "Work has been eating into my personal time" beats "I am satisfied with my work-life balance".
- Member register: flat, specific, no therapy or coach words, no "rate", "satisfied", "balance", "wellbeing", "purpose", "mindset", "values".
- Past four weeks, said once at the start of Part 1 and on each area title ("Work, the last four weeks").
- Plain international English, British spelling, numerals. Faith only as one example the member can bring.
- Written originally. No items copied from PERMA, SWLS, QOLI or other scales (rights in R1 section F).

## Scoring

- **Area reading (the wheel):** the 4 statements scored 0-4 (cost items reversed), plus the one-word check mapped to 0-4, averaged and shown as a slice length. At least 3 answers needed to draw an area; fewer, or skipped, stays missing. No overall life score, grade or percentile. Method published on the landing page.
- **Suggested focus:** among the areas the person marked as mattering this quarter, the one with the lowest reading. Shown with its reason ("You said Money matters this quarter, and it's where your answers were lowest"). The person can choose any area instead. If they marked nothing, suggest the lowest reading and say so.
- **Circle read (Part 4):** three short patterns, each triggered only by the person's own answers, e.g. "You're usually the one chasing", "Your goals live in your head", "You come back, quietly". Patterns describe, never diagnose. These are the shareable, Bestday-only part of the result.

## The result

1. The wheel of figures (kept from v2), built from their readings.
2. Per area, one or two lines quoting their own answers back ("You said work has been eating into your personal time, and that you're still not sure it's the right direction").
3. What matters this quarter and the suggested focus, with the reason.
4. The circle read.
5. The one thing: goal sentence, first step, day, and who sees it. Then Start your circle.

Gate (unchanged decision A2): the wheel, the suggested focus and one area read are free; the rest opens with first name and email.

## What we gain and lose by making it Bestday's

| | Gain | Lose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Member voice instead of the usual 0-10 wheel | Recognition: people see their own life in the questions; it reads like a friend asking, not a form | Searchers for "wheel of life" expect 1-10 ratings; we still give them a wheel, but not the classic exercise. A coach can't print it as the standard worksheet |
| Situational items instead of a validated scale | Specific, quotable results; no licensing questions | We cannot call it validated or compare results with research norms. We must say plainly it is a reflection tool |
| The circle section | The one thing no competitor has; leads straight to the product; shareable patterns | Adds 6 questions; may feel like a pitch if written badly |
| About 8 minutes instead of 3 | Depth, credibility, a result worth an email | Fewer people finish. Mitigation: sections, one-tap pace, and the wheel free before the gate |
| Audience-specific situations (moving abroad, family obligations, work on another country's hours) | It feels written for them | Less universal for US/UK searchers outside the diaspora; keep most items universal and a few specific |
